When the power goes off, and when it comes back

Коли Є shows planned outage schedules for your region and outage group: the status right now, a countdown to the next change and a 24-hour strip for today and tomorrow. Free, no account, works offline.

Windows 10 and 11, Android 8.0 and newer.

Unofficial app, not affiliated with DTEK, YASNO, oblenergos or government bodies. The official schedule is always on your supplier's website.

Everything the widget does

  • Region and group, not addresses

    Choose your region and outage group once, for example 1.1 or 3.2.

  • Up to 10 places

    Home, work, parents: each with its own region and group.

  • Status at a glance

    Power on, off or maybe off, shown by colour and text, with a large countdown to the next change.

  • Today and tomorrow

    A 24-hour strip labelled with the real switch times, not an even scale.

  • Three views

    Strip, Card and Ring. On top of all windows or on the desktop under them.

  • Notifications

    Before an outage, before power returns, when the schedule changes and when tomorrow is published. Quiet hours at night.

  • Works offline

    The last schedule is kept and marked with how old it is.

  • Light and tiny

    An installer of a couple of megabytes. Ukrainian, English and Russian.

Where the schedules come from

  1. Suppliers publish

    Each oblenergo publishes its schedule on its own site, in its own format.

  2. Open parsers collect

    Every 10 minutes they fetch the schedules, check them and publish one common format.

  3. Apps read one file

    The widget and the app never contact suppliers. They read the published file and keep the last one for offline use.

If a source fails, the last good schedule stays and the region is marked as possibly out of date.

Questions

Is this an official app?

No. Коли Є is unofficial and is not affiliated with DTEK, YASNO, any oblenergo or government body. Schedules may be late or differ from the official ones. Check your supplier's website for the official information.

Where do the schedules come from?

From suppliers' public websites and APIs. Open-source parsers collect them every 10 minutes; you can read the code and the status of every source on GitHub.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. The last downloaded schedule stays on the device and shows how old it is.

Does it collect any data?

No accounts, no analytics and no ads. It only downloads the public schedule files.
